What to Do When Customers Order but Don't Pay

Updated 24 October 2026 · 4 min read

An order is not a sale until the money is in your account. Here is how to cut down on customers who order and disappear.

Set the rule before the order

State your payment terms where customers see them: in your posts, bio and order form. For example: "Orders are confirmed after payment" or "Pay within 24 hours to hold your item."

Use deposits for bigger or custom orders

For made-to-order items, ask for part payment up front. It protects you from spending money and time on something the customer then abandons.

Follow up quickly and politely

  1. Send a friendly reminder a few hours after the order.
  2. Send a second reminder before the deadline.
  3. After the deadline, release the item and tell the customer it is back on sale.

Keep track of who has paid

List your orders as unpaid, paid and delivered. Review the unpaid ones every day so you can follow up while the customer still remembers.

Be careful with pay on delivery

Pay on delivery can work in a small area you know well. Across cities it adds the risk of refused deliveries and return costs, so many sellers ask for payment first.

Confirm the credit in your own account before dispatch. See how to confirm bank transfers.
