How to Cut Down "Where Is My Order?" Messages

Updated 24 October 2026 · 4 min read

Most "where is my order?" messages come from silence. When customers know what is happening, they stop asking.

Tell them what happens next

As soon as an order is placed, tell the customer the steps and rough timeline: payment confirmation, packing, dispatch, delivery.

Update at key moments

  1. Order received
  2. Payment confirmed
  3. Item dispatched, with the rider or courier details
  4. Item delivered

Four short messages beat one long chase later.

Set realistic delivery times

Promise what you can keep. A slightly longer, honest estimate causes fewer complaints than a short one you miss.

Keep delivery information handy

Save rider numbers, tracking details and dispatch dates next to each order so you can answer in seconds.

Create a short FAQ

Put delivery areas, typical times and your policy on delays in a highlight or pinned note. Many questions get answered before they are asked.

If something is delayed, say so early. Customers forgive delays more easily than silence.
