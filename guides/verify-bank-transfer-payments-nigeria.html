How to Confirm Bank Transfers and Avoid Fake Alerts

Updated 24 October 2026 · 4 min read

A screenshot only shows what a customer wants you to see. Here is how to be sure the money is actually in your account.

Why screenshots are not enough

Screenshots can be edited, reused, or show a transfer that is still pending or was reversed. Fake transfer apps also exist. Treat a screenshot as a claim, not as payment.

Checks that protect you

  1. Check your own bank app or SMS alert. Look for the credit in your account, not just a notification forwarded by someone else.
  2. Match the amount exactly. A short payment is not a paid order.
  3. Match the date and time to when the customer says they paid.
  4. Check the sender name or reference against the customer or order.
  5. Watch for repeats. The same transfer should not be used to claim two orders.
  6. Release the item only after the credit is confirmed.

Be careful with a customer who rushes you to ship because "the money has left my account". Pending transfers can fail.

Using your bank statement

When you have many orders, check payments in batches against your statement. Download it as a PDF from your bank app, then tick off each incoming credit against an order. Mark unclear ones for a closer look.

Keep a simple record

Next to each order, note the amount, the date and whether you confirmed it. If a dispute comes up later, you will know what happened.
