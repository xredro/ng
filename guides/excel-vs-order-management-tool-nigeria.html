Spreadsheet vs Order Management Tool for Small Sellers

Updated 24 October 2026 · 4 min read

A spreadsheet is a fine place to start. It is free, flexible and you already know it. The question is when it starts costing you time.

When a spreadsheet is enough

  • You take a handful of orders a week.
  • You are the only person handling them.
  • You are comfortable keeping it updated.

Where spreadsheets struggle

  • Retyping. Every order must be copied from a chat into a row.
  • Payment proof. Screenshots stay in chats, away from the order.
  • Totals and mistakes. Typos and broken formulas go unnoticed.
  • Phone use. Editing a big sheet on a phone is slow.
  • Customers. They cannot enter their own details.

What an order management tool changes

Customers fill in the details themselves, totals calculate automatically, and each order carries its status and payment proof. You manage orders instead of typing them.

Signs it is time to switch

  1. You spend a noticeable part of your day copying orders.
  2. You have lost or duplicated an order.
  3. You cannot quickly find a payment screenshot.
  4. You run drops where many orders arrive within hours.

You can keep a spreadsheet for accounting and use a tool for orders. They do not conflict.
